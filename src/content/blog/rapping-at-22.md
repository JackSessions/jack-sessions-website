I'm 22, I work in security, and I rap. People tend to treat those as two separate lives. They aren't, and I'd like to explain why.

## Writing is investigating

A good verse and a good incident report have more in common than you'd think. Both take a mess of raw material and turn it into something clear. Both need a structure, an ending that lands, and nothing in them that doesn't earn its place. When I write bars, I edit the way I edit a timeline: cut anything that doesn't support the point.

## Performing made the talks better

Before my first conference talk I'd already spent time in front of people with a mic. That helped more than any presentation course. You learn to read a room, to keep going when something breaks, and to let a bit of personality through. My talks have memes, soundboards and live demos because I'd rather people enjoy the technical content than sit through it.

## The scene doesn't always get it

Some people in security think music is a distraction. Some people in music find the security work strange. I've stopped trying to explain it to either side. Both come from the same habit: I like making things, and I like showing how they work.

## What's next

I'll keep releasing music and I'll keep putting it on this site. You can find it on the [music page](/music), and on [YouTube](/videos) if you'd rather watch.

If you make something on the side that doesn't "fit" your job, keep going. It's probably making you better at the job.
