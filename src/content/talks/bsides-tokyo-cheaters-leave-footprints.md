Cheating in modern competitive games is no longer just a gameplay issue — it is a digital forensics problem.

Using popular multiplayer titles in Asia — League of Legends, Valorant, and Apex Legends — this talk examines how cheats behave like malware implants and how they leave persistent forensic artefacts across memory, disk and telemetry.

The session focuses on **post-incident analysis** rather than cheat development:

- How cheating activity can be reconstructed after the fact
- How claims of innocence can be validated or disproven against the artefact timeline
- Why anti-cheat engineering closely mirrors modern endpoint detection and response systems

The talk is aimed at DFIR practitioners and security researchers who want to understand why the same techniques used to investigate malware apply directly to the gaming abuse space.
